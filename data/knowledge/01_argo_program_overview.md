# ARGO Program Overview & Global Observing Array

## 1. Mission and Scientific Objectives
The international Argo program is a major component of the Global Ocean Observing System (GOOS), co-sponsored by the World Meteorological Organization (WMO) and the Intergovernmental Oceanographic Commission (IOC) of UNESCO. 

The primary mission of Argo is to maintain a continuous, real-time, high-resolution global array of autonomous profiling floats. Argo monitors the physical state of the upper 2,000 meters of the ocean, measuring temperature, salinity, and subsurface ocean velocity.

Key objectives include:
- Quantifying global ocean heat uptake and spatial distribution to understand global warming.
- Tracking changes in the global hydrological cycle through accurate salinity measurements.
- Providing operational upper-ocean data assimilation for seasonal climate forecasting, coupled ocean-atmosphere models, and tropical cyclone intensity prediction.
- Providing open-access oceanographic datasets without delay or proprietary restrictions.

## 2. Global Array Scale and International Collaboration
The initial Argo milestone of 3,000 active floats was achieved in November 2007. Today, the global array typically maintains approximately 4,000 active robotic floats distributed across all oceans, roughly on a 3-degree by 3-degree grid (~300 km spacing).

Over 30 nations deploy and maintain Argo floats. Major contributing national programs include:
- United States (US Argo / NOAA / Scripps Institution of Oceanography / Woods Hole Oceanographic Institution / University of Washington)
- India (INCOIS - Indian National Centre for Ocean Information Services / Ministry of Earth Sciences)
- France (Coriolis / Ifremer)
- Japan (JAMSTEC - Japan Agency for Marine-Earth Science and Technology)
- United Kingdom (UK Met Office / BODC)
- Australia (CSIRO / IMOS)
- Canada (DFO - Fisheries and Oceans Canada)
- European Union (Euro-Argo ERIC)

## 3. Expansion: Deep Argo and BGC-Argo
The core Argo mission covers 0 to 2,000 meters depth. The program has expanded into two critical frontiers:

1. **Deep Argo**:
   - Specialized floats (e.g., Deep SOLO, Deep APEX) engineered with spherical glass or thick ceramic pressure housings.
   - Capable of diving to 4,000 or 6,000 meters depth.
   - Designed to measure bottom-water warming, abyssal ocean heat uptake, and global thermohaline overturning circulation changes.

2. **Biogeochemical Argo (BGC-Argo)**:
   - Floats equipped with bio-optical and chemical sensors measuring 6 core ocean health variables:
     - Dissolved Oxygen (O2)
     - Nitrate (NO3)
     - pH (ocean acidification)
     - Chlorophyll-a fluorescence (phytoplankton biomass)
     - Suspended particles / optical backscattering
     - Downwelling irradiance
   - Critical for studying oceanic carbon uptake, deoxygenation, and marine ecosystem dynamics.
